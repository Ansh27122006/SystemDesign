Fault Tolerance

The ability to keep working — fully or partially — when a component fails, rather than failing completely. Three patterns that show up in almost every real architecture: automatic failover, circuit breakers, and bulkhead isolation.

A Automatic Failover & Split-Brain

A standby node watches the primary's heartbeat. Miss enough beats and it promotes itself. That's great — until the old primary comes back not knowing it was replaced, and you end up with two nodes both convinced they're in charge.

B Circuit Breakers — Fail Fast Instead of Hanging

Make Service B unhealthy and watch what happens to Service A's callers. Without a breaker, every request still waits the full timeout on a call that's doomed anyway. With one, repeated failures trip the breaker open and further calls fail instantly — protecting A from B's problems.

Three patterns, one goal

PatternProtects againstTrade-off
Automatic failoverTotal loss of a single nodeNeeds careful fencing, or risks split-brain
Circuit breakerCascading slowdown from a failing dependencyFails fast even for requests that might have succeeded
Bulkhead isolationOne overloaded component starving all the othersCosts more resources — capacity can't be shared across features
The common thread: fault tolerance never prevents failures — it limits their blast radius and their duration. Every pattern here trades some efficiency (idle standby capacity, fast-failed requests, dedicated-but-sometimes-idle pools) for a system that degrades instead of collapsing.
