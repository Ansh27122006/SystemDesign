System Design Lab · Characteristic 2 of 8

Reliability

Not "is it up right now" — "does it keep doing the right thing, every time, without failing"?

00 · The distinction

Available ≠ Reliable

These two get confused constantly. They measure completely different things.

Availability The friend who always picks up the phone — but half the time gives you wrong directions. Always reachable. Not trustworthy.
Reliability The friend who's sometimes unreachable — but when they do answer, the advice is always correct. Harder to reach. Never wrong.

02 · Building it

How reliability is engineered

Availability is fixed mostly with redundancy. Reliability needs a different toolbox — one focused on correctness, not just uptime.

🗳️

Replication + quorum

Store data on multiple nodes; require a majority to agree before trusting a value.

✅

Checksums / validation

Detect silent data corruption before it's ever served to a user.

🔁

Retries + idempotency

Automatically retry transient failures safely, without causing duplicate side-effects.

🧯

Circuit breakers

Stop calling a failing dependency so errors don't cascade through the system.

🪜

Graceful degradation

Serve a slightly reduced experience instead of a hard failure when something's off.

🐒

Chaos engineering

Deliberately break things in production to prove the system survives real failures.

04 · Handling transient failure

Retries turn a blip into a non-event

Most real-world failures are transient — a dropped packet, a momentary timeout. A reliable client just tries again.

05 · The formula

Reliability as a probability

Assuming failures happen at a roughly constant rate, the probability a system survives an entire window t without failing is R(t) = e^(−t / MTBF). Longer MTBF or shorter windows both push reliability up.
