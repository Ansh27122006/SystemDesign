Consistency

Whether every read sees the most recent write — or might see something older. It's the characteristic most directly in tension with availability and latency. Three scenes: replication style, the quorum math behind it, and what happens when the network itself breaks.

A Synchronous vs. Asynchronous Replication

Write to the primary, then immediately read from a replica. In strong (synchronous) mode the write waits for every replica before confirming. In eventual (asynchronous) mode the write returns instantly and replicas catch up — meaning a read can briefly return something stale.

B Quorum Consistency — Tuning N, W, R

With N replicas, a write is confirmed once W of them accept it; a read is confirmed once R of them respond, returning the newest version seen. The classic guarantee: if W + R > N, every read quorum is mathematically guaranteed to overlap the last write quorum — no stale reads are possible. Drag the sliders to break that guarantee.

C CAP Theorem: When the Network Splits

Two clusters, one link between them. Cut the link and you must choose: reject requests to stay consistent (CP), or keep serving both sides and risk them disagreeing (AP). You can't have all three of consistency, availability, and partition tolerance at once — partitions happen, so this is really a C-vs-A choice during one.

Why this is the hardest characteristic to get right: consistency isn't free — every scene above traded it against latency or availability somewhere. Real systems rarely pick one model globally; they pick per operation. A bank balance read might demand strong consistency; a social media like-count is happy to be eventually consistent for the sake of speed.
